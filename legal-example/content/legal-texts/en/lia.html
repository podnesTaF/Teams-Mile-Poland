1. Subject of the test

This Balance Test is a one-off document, prepared jointly for the entire series of events "Ace Battle Run Polska – Qualification for one mile" organized in 2026 at the "Podskarbińska" Stadium in Warsaw and for their subsequent editions, as long as the method of data processing and the scope of published results remain identical to those described below. A separate, short "Confirmation of the application of the LIA to the event" is prepared for each edition, which does not replace this document, but refers to it.

This document is an analysis of the admissibility of the processing of personal data of event participants:

Ace Battle Run Poland – One Mile Qualifier

Warsaw, "Podskarbińska" Stadium - all editions organized in 2026 (27.06.2026, 01.08.2026, 08.08.2026, 15.08.2026, 22.08.2026, 29.08.2026)
and subsequent editions of the event organized by the Administrator on the same terms.

The subject of the analysis is:

making the official results of the competition publicly available on the Internet, without the need for registration or login of the user.

2. Legal basis

The planned processing operation shall be based, to the extent covered by this test, on:

art. 6 ust. 1 lit. f RODO

i.e. processing necessary for the purposes resulting from the legitimate interests of the Controller or a third party, provided that the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject are not of an overriding nature.

The Personal Data Protection Office indicates that the application of this basis requires a balancing test comprising three elements: a legitimate interest, the necessity of processing and a balancing of the interests and rights of the data subject.

4. First stage of the test – is there a legitimate interest?

4.1. Interest of the Administrator

The Controller identifies the following legitimate interests:

A. Proper documentation of the results of the competition

The sports result is the basic result of the event.

The organizer must be able to:

  • classification determination;

  • the preservation of the official result;

  • demonstrating the result;

  • consideration of protests;

  • correction of errors.

B. Transparency of competition

The public classification allows participants to verify:

  • own score;

  • place;

  • the results of other participants;

  • the correctness of the order.

C. Credibility of the event

The public results make it possible to demonstrate that the competition was actually carried out and that the classification was prepared in a verifiable manner.

D. Documenting the history of sporting events

The Organizer has a legitimate interest in keeping information about the competition and its results.

E. Interest of the participants

The interest in publishing the results does not concern only the Administrator.

Participants themselves may be interested in:

  • public confirmation of the achieved result;

  • comparison of results;

  • demonstrating sporting achievement;

  • access to the official classification.

5. Is the interest "legitimate"?

YES.

Interest:

  • is real;

  • is not against the law;

  • is specific;

  • results directly from the nature of the sports competitions organized;

  • concerns the proper documentation and communication of the results of the event.

In addition, the current decision of the Personal Data Protection Office concerning sports competitions confirms that the publication of data of competition participants may be an acceptable action of the organizer, if the scope of data is appropriate for the purpose.

6. Second stage – is publication necessary?

6.1. Purpose

The goal is to make the official classification publicly available.

To achieve this goal, you need first of all:

name + surname + sports result.

Depending on the rules of the competition, you may also need:

  • place;

  • category;

  • points.

7. Can the same goal be achieved by a less intrusive measure?

The administrator has considered the following alternatives.

Alternative 1 – publication only for logged-in users

Rating: insufficient.

The registration requirement limits access to official results.

Those interested in the results would have to:

  • create an account;

  • Provide data

  • remember a password;

  • Go through the login process.

This solution is not necessary for the usual viewing of official sports results.

Alternative 2 – publication of only start numbers

Rating: insufficient.

The start number does not allow third parties to easily determine which competitor a given result belongs to.

This reduces the transparency of the classification and makes it difficult for participants to verify the results.

Alternative 3 – Publish initials instead of first and last name

Rating: partially useful, but not sufficient.

Initials limit the identification of the participant, but at the same time make it difficult to:

  • verification of the result;

  • confirmation of the correctness of the classification;

  • finding your own result;

  • distinguishing players with similar data.

Alternative 4 – public results including name, surname and result

Assessment: appropriate and proportionate.

The solution provides:

  • identification of the participant;

  • the ability to verify the result;

  • transparency of classification;

while limiting the scope of the data.

8. Why is access without registration justified?

The Administrator recognizes that the lack of the obligation to register is an element of achieving the goal of public availability of results, and not an additional marketing goal.

Public availability means the opportunity to get acquainted with the official results by:

  • players;

  • family and friends;

  • trainers;

  • clubs;

  • people who work with athletes;

  • organizers of other competitions;

  • Performance verifiers.

The introduction of mandatory login would create an additional barrier that is not necessary to protect the data of participants if a minimum amount of data is published at the same time.

The Administrator does not require any personal data from the person viewing the results.

9. Third stage – balancing interests

It should be assessed whether the interest of the Administrator and the participants in the public publication of the results is not outweighed by the rights and freedoms of the participants.

9.1. Nature of the data

The following will be published:

  • name;

  • surname;

  • time;

  • place/ranking.

This data does not belong to the special categories of personal data under Article 9 of the GDPR.

The following will not be published:

  • health data;

  • PESEL;

  • address;

  • telephone;

  • e-mail;

  • Date of birth.

10. Reasonable expectations of the participant

A participant entering a sports competition can reasonably expect that his sports result will be:

  • measured;

  • classified;

  • documented;

  • presented in the official results of the competition.

This is especially important in a situation where, before participating, the participant receives the Terms and Conditions, which clearly state:

"The official results of the Competition may be published on the Organizer's website in a publicly accessible manner for all Internet users, without the need to register or log in."

The participant is therefore informed before participation, not only after publication.

11. Risk to participants

The administrator identifies the following potential risks:

  1. the possibility of finding the result by third parties;

  2. the ability to copy publicly available data;

  3. the possibility of combining the name and surname with the sports result;

  4. the ability to index the website by search engines.

The risk is considered limited because only data related to sports performance is published.

No contact details or particularly sensitive data are published.

12. Risk minimization measures

The Administrator undertakes to apply the following measures:

12.1. Data minimization

Only the following will be published:

name + surname + result + place/ranking + possible category.

12.2. No contact details

They will never be published:

  • e-mail;

  • telephone;

  • address.

12.3. No sensitive data

The following will not be published:

  • health data;

  • PESEL;

  • contact person's details.

12.4. No hidden data

The results publication system must generate a separate public dataset and not share the entire participant database.

12.5. File Control

An administrator should not publish Excel/CSV/PDF files that contain hidden columns or additional metadata if they may allow access to data that is not intended for publication.

12.6. API Control

If the results are retrieved by the API, the public endpoint should only return fields intended for publishing.

For example:

firstName
lastName
time
rank
category

and should not return:

email
phone
dateOfBirth
address
emergencyContact
healthData

13. Particular significance of the decision of the Personal Data Protection Office DKN.5131.58.2022

The Administrator took into account the decision of the President of the Personal Data Protection Office of 30 April 2024, ref. no. DKN.5131.58.2022, concerning the publication of data of participants in sports competitions.

In this case, the UODO stated that the organizer had the right to intentionally publish the data of the participants of the competition, while the violation consisted in the fact that as a result of the erroneous sharing of the file, third parties also gained access to e-mail addresses and dates of birth.

As a consequence, the Administrator assumes as a rule:

Public Sports Score ≠ a public database of participants.

This is a key principle of this LIA.

14. Balance Test Result

After conducting the analysis, the Administrator states that:

THE INTEREST IS LEGALLY JUSTIFIED

YES.

PROCESSING IS NECESSARY FOR THE PURSUIT OF THIS INTEREST

YES, in terms of making publicly available the minimum set of data needed to identify a sports score.

THERE IS A LESS INTRUSIVE MEASURE THAT PRODUCES THE SAME RESULT

Not within the scope of the objective of genuinely public, easily accessible rankings, if the alternative would require logging in or limit the possibility of independent verification of results.

DO THE RIGHTS AND FREEDOMS OF THE PARTICIPANTS PREVAIL?

No – with the described restrictions and safeguards.

15. Final decision of the Administrator

The Administrator acknowledges that:

publication of the participant's name, surname, time of the run and place/ranking in a publicly available leaderboard, without the need to register or log in, may be carried out on the basis of Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR, provided that the scope of data specified in this LIA and the Terms and Conditions is maintained and appropriate technical and organizational measures are applied.

At the same time, the Administrator does not consider the following to be covered by this interest:

  • Email address

  • Your phone number

  • address;

  • date of birth;

  • PESEL;

  • emergency contact details;

  • health information.

16. Opposition procedure

If the participant objects pursuant to Article 21 of the GDPR, the Controller:

  1. registers an objection;

  2. identifies the scope of processing to which the objection relates;

  3. analyses the reasons indicated by the person, if required;

  4. reassess the existence of overriding legitimate grounds;

  5. decides on further processing;

  6. documents the decision;

  7. informs the person about the manner in which the objection is to be considered.
