§ 1. General Provisions

  1. These Regulations define the rules for the organization and conduct of the ACE BATTLE RUN – TEAM MILE POLAND sports competition, in the format of team–individual ranking competition over a distance of one mile (hereinafter: the “Competition” or the “Event”).

  2. These Regulations constitute the primary public document defining the rules of participation in the Competition, organization of starts, time measurement, determination of results, submission of protests, establishment of classifications, as well as the rules for publication of results.

  3. The official source of information and registration related to the Event is the platform:

poland.acebattle.run

(hereinafter: the “Platform”).

  1. The Organizer of the Competition within the meaning of Polish law is:

  1. The initiator of the international ACE BATTLE RUN series is Ace Battle Association (ABA), Luxembourg (NPO), where this follows from the rules governing the operation of the ACE BATTLE RUN system.

  2. The Event Director is the person designated by the Organizer and published on the Platform or in the Event documentation.

  3. The Competition shall be conducted in accordance with these Regulations, the relevant ACE BATTLE RUN sports regulations, as well as mandatory provisions of law.

  4. In the event of any conflict between the provisions of these Regulations and mandatory provisions of law, the provisions of law shall apply.

§ 3. Registration and Confirmation of Participation

  1. Registration is mandatory for a person seeking to participate in the Competition, as well as to obtain a result and ranking status in the ACE BATTLE RUN RATING system.

  2. The Participant shall complete individual registration via the Platform and indicate a team or level in accordance with the rules applicable to the relevant format.

  3. Team registration may be completed by the Team Captain or an authorized representative of the Team, in particular the Team Manager.

  4. In exceptional cases, the Organizer may allow individual registration on the day of the Competition, no later than 30 minutes before the commencement of the relevant race, provided that this is technically and organizationally possible.

  5. Participation shall be confirmed upon:

  1. completion of the required registration;

  2. acceptance of the applicable regulations;

  3. obtaining the Confirmed status;

  4. obtaining QR confirmation.

  1. Prior registration alone does not constitute a guarantee of admission to the start.

  2. The Organizer may refuse admission to the start in the event of failure to meet the conditions specified in the Regulations.

§ 4. Check-in and Participant Verification

  1. Team check-in shall, as a rule, begin one hour before the commencement of the relevant race, in accordance with the current schedule.

  2. The Team Captain or Team Manager confirms the team composition designated for the relevant race.

  3. The Organizer may verify the identity of a Participant on the basis of an identity document.

  4. The Participant is obliged to ensure that the data presented during verification corresponds to the data provided during registration.

  5. Following successful verification, the Participant shall receive:

  1. a race number;

  2. a chip or other device intended for time measurement;

  3. an identification marking necessary for the proper conduct of the race.

  1. The chip intended for time measurement shall be placed in accordance with the Organizer’s instructions.

  2. The Team Captain determines the roles of athletes within the race in accordance with the rules applicable to the relevant format.

  3. Roles may be designated as follows:

  • RACER — white marking;
  • ACE — red marking;
  • JOKER — black marking.
  1. The Organizer may use another technical designation of roles if necessary for the proper conduct of the Competition.

§ 5. Closing of Check-in

  1. Registration and check-in for the relevant race shall, as a rule, close 15 minutes before its commencement.

  2. Following the closing of check-in, new entries for the relevant race shall not be accepted, except in cases expressly announced by the Organizer.

  3. A Participant who has not completed the required check-in procedure may be assigned the status DNS (Did Not Start) and may not be admitted to the start.

  4. The Participant is obliged to comply with the instructions of the Organizer, judges, starter, safety personnel and venue staff.

§ 6. Event Schedule

  1. The Event includes, in particular, the following stages:

  1. technical preparation of the venue;

  2. opening of the secretariat and time measurement system;

  3. activation of medical safety provision;

  4. registration and check-in of Participants;

  5. verification of data;

  6. issuance of race numbers and chips;

  7. formation of starting groups;

  8. conduct of races;

  9. publication of preliminary results;

  10. acceptance and consideration of protests;

  11. publication of official results;

  12. closing of the Event.

  1. The detailed timing for individual elements is set out in Annexes 1 and 2 to these Regulations and published by the Organizer on the Platform.

  2. In the event of a delay to the schedule, deadlines dependent on the course of the Event shall be calculated from the actual time at which the relevant event occurs.

  3. The Organizer may change the order or timing of individual elements if justified by organizational or technical reasons or safety considerations.

§ 7. Starting Slots and Call Room

  1. The planned interval between successive races is 15 minutes.

  2. Indicative schedule:

  1. Participants of the subsequent race may be called to the Call Room simultaneously with the conduct of the preceding race.

  2. The Participant should arrive at the Call Room approximately 10 minutes before the scheduled start.

  3. Approximately 3 minutes before the start, Participants should be ready to enter the track.

  4. Entry onto the track shall take place exclusively upon the instruction of a judge or an authorized person.

  5. Participants of the subsequent race may not enter the track or the finish area before their official admission.

  6. The subsequent start may take place only after:

  7. the track has been cleared by the preceding group;

  8. the readiness of the time measurement system has been checked;

  9. readiness has been confirmed by the starter.

§ 8. Team Format and Ranking

  1. The team ranking race is intended to determine the team result and its ranking level.

  2. Each Participant receives an individual result and a position in the relevant classification.

  3. A Participant’s result may constitute the basis for obtaining TEAM RUNNER status in the ACE BATTLE RUN RATING system in accordance with the rules of that system.

  4. Participation in subsequent projects, including ACE BATTLE MILE CITY LEAGUE (WARSAW), may depend on meeting additional criteria specified in separate regulations.

  5. Obtaining PLAYER status in the international ACE BATTLE RUN system shall take place exclusively in accordance with the rules applicable to that system.

  6. The individual classification shall be conducted separately for women and men.

  7. The team classification shall be conducted in the following categories:

  1. men’s teams;

  2. women’s teams;

  3. mixed teams.

  1. The rules for dividing teams into levels and divisions are specified by the relevant ACE BATTLE RUN RATING rules and Annexes 4 and 5.

§ 9. Race Rules

  1. The start shall take place from a standing position, from a common curved starting line.

  2. After the start, a Participant may take a more advantageous position on the track, provided that this does not result in:

  3. pushing;

  1. blocking;

  2. deliberately obstructing the race;

  3. creating a danger to other Participants.

  1. The following are prohibited:
  1. shortening the distance;
  1. leaving the designated course in order to gain an advantage;
  1. using unauthorized assistance from third parties;
  1. using a race number assigned to another person;
  1. any other conduct violating the principles of fair play.

  1. A Participant who commits a second false start in a manner resulting in the application of a sanction provided for by the applicable sports rules may be disqualified.

  2. The decision concerning a violation of the starting rules shall be made by the competent judge.

§ 10. Time Measurement

  1. The official time measurement system during the Competition is the Race Result system.

  2. Each Participant is identified by means of an assigned race number and a measuring device/chip.

  3. The Participant receives an individual time and a position in the relevant classification.

  4. In the event of a failure or doubts concerning electronic time measurement, the Organizer may use supporting data, in particular:

  5. manual time measurement;

  1. the order of crossing the finish line determined by the judges;

  2. photographic record;

  3. video recording;

  1. other available technical data.

  2. In the event of discrepancies in the data, the competent judge shall assess the available evidence, taking into account, in particular, the race number, Participant data, time, order of crossing the finish line and sports category.

§ 11. Control Time and Result Statuses

  1. The planned control time for completing the race is from 4 to 10 minutes, depending on the sporting level of the group.

  2. For ranking qualification purposes, the Participant must complete the distance within the time specified for the relevant race or level.

  3. A Participant who does not complete the distance within the maximum control time of 10 minutes may be asked by the judge to safely leave the track and may receive DNF status.

  4. The results system uses, in particular, the following statuses:

  • FINISHED — the Participant completed the full distance and the result was recorded;
  • DNS (Did Not Start) — the Participant did not start the race, including due to failure to complete the required check-in;
  • DNF (Did Not Finish) — the Participant started the race but did not complete the distance;
  • DSQ (Disqualified) — the Participant was disqualified on the basis of a judge’s decision;
  • UNDER REVIEW — the result is undergoing verification.

§ 12. Results, Protests and Appeals

  1. The preliminary result of the relevant race shall be published, where technically possible, no later than 10 minutes after the actual completion of the race.

  2. A protest may concern only a specific error or violation that affected the result.

  3. A protest may concern, in particular:

  1. incorrect identification of a Participant or race number;

  1. an error in Race Result time measurement;

  2. an error in the order of crossing the finish line;

  3. incorrect counting of laps;

  4. obstruction, pushing or blocking;

  5. shortening of the distance;

  1. an incorrect DNS, DNF or DSQ status;

  2. an incorrect category;

  3. a violation of the starting procedure capable of affecting the result.

  1. Mere dissatisfaction with the time or place obtained, without indicating a specific error or violation, does not constitute grounds for an effective protest.

  2. A protest must be submitted within 30 minutes from the time of publication of the preliminary result, unless the Organizer announces a different deadline for a particular race for technical reasons.

  3. A protest should contain:

  1. race number;
  1. race bib number;

  1. details of the person submitting the protest;

  2. a description of the disputed event or result;

  3. identification of the specific error or violation;

  1. the requested decision.

  2. A protest may be submitted by the Participant, the Team Captain or another person authorized in accordance with the Competition Regulations.

  3. The submission of a protest does not suspend the running of the programme.

  4. If a decision requires analysis of technical data, video recording or other materials, the result may be assigned UNDER REVIEW status.

  5. Results that are not disputed may be published without waiting for the resolution of a matter concerning another result.

  6. Following consideration of a protest, the result and ranking positions dependent on it may be corrected accordingly.

  7. The decision concerning the protest shall be communicated to the person who submitted it.

  8. An appeal against a decision concerning a protest may be submitted within 24 hours from its delivery, unless the relevant sports regulations provide otherwise.

  9. An appeal may be considered by a panel consisting of three persons who did not participate in making the original decision.

  10. An appeal does not suspend the Competition programme.

  11. The final ranking shall be approved after completion of the procedure for considering protests and appeals.

§ 13. Individual Ranking

  1. The individual ranking shall take into account the Participant’s best official result obtained during the relevant ACE BATTLE RUN ranking period.

  2. In the event of equal best results, the second-best result shall be taken into account.

  3. If the second result does not allow a decision to be made, the earlier achievement of the best result may be decisive.

  4. The individual result may determine the Participant’s level in accordance with the ACE BATTLE RUN RATING rules.

  5. Detailed ranking rules are specified in Annex 4 and may additionally be established in separate regulations of the ranking system.

  3. The planned medical point shall remain available during the relevant part of the Event in accordance with the schedule and organization of medical safety provision.

  4. Depending on availability, the safety provision may include, in particular:

  5. basic medical equipment;

  1. an AED;

  1. first-aid supplies;

  2. the possibility of calling emergency services at 112;

  3. water and a place for rest or recovery.

  1. The Participant is obliged to immediately stop the race if further participation could endanger their health or safety.
  1. The person responsible for medical safety may recommend or order the discontinuation of the athlete’s further participation if justified by the athlete’s health condition or safety considerations.
  1. The Event Director, Chief Judge or another authorized person may interrupt, delay or cancel the start for safety reasons.
  1. The Participant is obliged to comply with the instructions of medical personnel and persons responsible for safety.
